# Contributing to HausaSafe

HausaSafe exists because AI safety infrastructure has been built almost
entirely in English. Closing that gap for Hausa — and eventually other
underrepresented languages — depends on contributions from people who
actually speak the language and understand the communities affected.

You do not need a technical or AI background to contribute. The most
valuable contributions right now are linguistic and cultural, not
technical.

---

## Ways to contribute

### 1. Translate an existing prompt or glossary entry

The fastest way to help. Many entries in `/evaluation-prompts` and
`/glossary` are currently English-only, marked with `[translation needed]`.

- Pick one item from any category file.
- Provide a natural, community-register Hausa translation — not overly
  formal or literary. These prompts are meant to reflect how these
  scenarios actually arrive in real life (WhatsApp messages, voice notes,
  conversations), so natural spoken register matters more than formal
  written Hausa.
- Open a Pull Request, or open an Issue with your suggested translation if
  you're not comfortable using Git directly.

### 2. Review an existing translation

Even entries marked as bilingual have not necessarily been reviewed by more
than one native speaker. If you notice a translation that feels unnatural,
imprecise, or regionally inconsistent (Hausa varies across northern
Nigeria, Niger, and the wider Sahel), please flag it.

- Open an Issue quoting the specific entry and explaining the concern.
- Suggested corrections are especially welcome.

### 3. Add a new documented translation gap

If you've encountered an AI or technology-related term that has no clear
Hausa equivalent — beyond what's already documented in
`/translation-gaps` — please add it.

- Describe the English term and its meaning.
- Note whether no equivalent exists, or whether an approximation is
  possible (and what that approximation is).
- Explain briefly why the gap matters in practice.

### 4. Propose a new evaluation prompt

If you can think of a realistic scenario — grounded in how AI-generated
misinformation, scams, or impersonation actually show up in Hausa-speaking
communities — that isn't yet covered in `/evaluation-prompts`, propose it.

- Describe the scenario and why it matters.
- Draft the prompt in English and, if possible, Hausa.
- Note what a model's response should and shouldn't do.

### 5. Share fieldwork or community feedback

If you have direct experience — as a health worker, community leader,
educator, or community member — with how misinformation, scams, or AI
impersonation actually reach people in your community, that context is
valuable even without a specific prompt or translation attached. Open an
Issue and share what you've observed.

---

## Who this project needs

- Native Hausa speakers, from any region or dialect background
- Community health workers and educators in northern Nigeria and the wider
  Sahel
- Linguists and NLP researchers working on Hausa or related languages
- Anyone with direct experience of the scenarios this project documents

---

## How review works

Given the sensitivity of some content (religious framing, health
misinformation, financial scam scripts), translations and new prompts
should ideally be reviewed by at least one other native Hausa speaker
before being marked as finalised. Reviewed entries will be marked
accordingly as this process matures.

---

## Code of conduct

This project deals with real harms — misinformation, scams, impersonation
— aimed at real communities. Please engage in that spirit: contributions
should aim to protect and inform communities, not to refine harmful content
for other purposes. Disagreements about translation, framing, or approach
are welcome and expected; bad-faith contributions are not.
