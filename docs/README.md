# Docs

This folder collects the research, writing, and academic grounding behind
HausaSafe — for anyone who wants to understand the argument in more depth
than the main README covers, or who wants to cite this work properly.

---

## Foundational writing

**["Epistemic Exclusion of the Hausa Language in AI Safety"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/utZcwXd65yggubonn/epistemic-exclusion-of-the-hausa-language-in-ai-safety)**
Hadiya Usman, EA Forum, 2026.

The original piece laying out the argument behind this project: that AI
safety evaluations, red-teaming, and benchmarks are built almost
exclusively in English, and that this is a safety failure rather than a
representation issue. Documents the specific translation gaps (deepfakes,
prompt, alignment, and others) that motivated HausaSafe, and situates the
problem within the broader conversation about AI safety and the Global
South.

---

## Academic grounding

HausaSafe's approach is informed by and responds to:

**Ireri, Abungu et al. (2026).** Research on Africa-centric AI safety
evaluation, arguing that low-resource, non-English-language contexts are
likely to expose model failure modes that English-language safety
evaluation cannot detect. HausaSafe treats this as a direct call to
action: if such failure modes exist, they cannot be found without
building the evaluation infrastructure to look for them — which is what
this project does for Hausa specifically.

## How HausaSafe fits into a broader research programme

HausaSafe is one part of an ongoing programme of work on AI safety and
governance in northern Nigeria, alongside:

- Field research on AI-related misinformation, scams, and impersonation
  patterns affecting Hausa-speaking communities (see
  `/evaluation-prompts` for the resulting prompt set).
- Community validation fieldwork using a structured KoboToolbox interview
  instrument, covering the same six scenario categories represented in
  `/evaluation-prompts`.
- Ongoing advocacy and engagement work connecting this research to AI
  governance and policy conversations in Nigeria and the broader region.
